# Malayalam sound inventory: the units to build once and combine into words

Status: planning note (2026-10-13). Part A is the inventory text supplied by the project owner, kept **unchanged**.
Part B adds corrections and gaps found while checking it. Part C has measurements on the 257,684-word lexicon.
Part D is the proposed plan. Nothing in Part A was removed or reworded.

### 3. Consonants (വ്യഞ്ജനാക്ഷരങ്ങൾ - Vyanjanaksharangal), 36 letters
A. Plosives / Stops (വല്ലി - Valli)
* Velar Stops (Throat sounds): ka (ക), kha (ഖ), ga (ഗ), gha (ഘ), nga (ങ)
* Palatal Stops (Palate sounds): ca (ച), cha (ഛ), ja (ജ), jha (ഝ), nja (ഞ)
* Retroflex Stops (Rolled-tongue sounds): ta (ട), tha (ഠ), da (ഡ), dha (ഢ), na (ണ)
* Dental Stops (Teeth-touching sounds): ta (ത), tha (ഥ), da (ദ), dha (ധ), na (ന)
* Labial Stops (Lip sounds): pa (പ), pha (ഫ), ba (ബ), bha (ഭ), ma (മ)

B. Approximants, Sibilants, and Liquids
* ya (യ), ra (ര), la (ല), va (വ)
* sha (ശ), sha (ഷ), sa (സ), ha (ഹ)
* la (ള), zha (ഴ), ra (റ)

### 4. Chillu Letters (ചില്ലക്ഷരങ്ങൾ), 5 letters
Pure consonants that do not take a vowel or require a chandrakala:
1. ൺ (na)
2. ൻ (n)
3. ർ (r)
4. ൽ (l)
5. ൾ (la)

### 5. la (ല), ൾ (la) or il?
1. ല (la). A regular, full consonant, used at the beginning or middle of words with vowels (ലാ, ലി, ലു). Example: ലഭ്യമാവുക (labhyamavuka, available).
2. ൾ (la / ḷa). A chillu letter (pure consonant), a retroflex lateral made with the tongue curled further back. It never takes a vowel sign. Example: ആൾ (aal, person).
3. ൽ (il / l). Also a chillu letter, a dental "l" like the 'l' at the end of "ball". Written out in Manglish standalone it often sounds like il or al. Example: കാൽ (kaal, leg).

Corrected Chillu list with accurate Manglish sounds:
1. ൺ (n / ṇ)
2. ൻ (n)
3. ർ (r)
4. ൽ (il / l)
5. ൾ (al / ḷ)

## Part B. Corrections and gaps found while checking (additions only)

1. **Vowel ഔ (au) is missing** from the vowel list (the sign ൗ is in the syllable list, but the independent letter ഔ is not). Modern Malayalam
   has 13 vowels: അ ആ ഇ ഈ ഉ ഊ ഋ എ ഏ ഐ ഒ ഓ ഔ. ഌ, ൠ, ൡ are archaic/rare. അം and അഃ are a vowel plus the signs ം (anusvara, "m") and ഃ (visarga, "h"),
   not separate vowels.
2. **Names are not unique**, so use the script letter as the unit identity, not the Manglish name: "ta" is both ട and ത; "na" is ണ and ന; "la" is ല and ള;
   "ra" is ര and റ; "sha" is ശ and ഷ; "kee" is both കീ and കേ; "koo" is both കൂ and കോ. Short and long e/o (എ/ഏ, ഒ/ഓ) differ in length.
   (The project's current rule-based unit names also merge ഏ with എ and ഓ with ഒ, and ഠ/ത/ഥ share "th": check this before using them as labels.)
3. **Chillu letters:** the five modern ones are ൺ ൻ ർ ൽ ൾ (ൿ is rare). They are pure consonants /ɳ n r l ɭ/; "il" and "al" are only how people
   say their names aloud, not the sound.
4. **The biggest gap: conjuncts and doubled consonants (കൂട്ടക്ഷരങ്ങൾ)**, for example ക്ക ങ്ക ഞ്ച ണ്ട ന്ത ന്ന മ്പ ല്ല റ്റ ന്റ ക്ഷ ത്ത. Real words are made of
   these far more than the 36 + 13 + 5 letters suggest (Part C).
5. **Word-final reduced vowel (samvruthokaram, written ു or ്)** is pronounced very short; a unit recorded alone will not sound like it does in a word.

## Part D. Proposed plan (not built yet)

1. Use the **akshara** (consonant + vowel as one unit) as the atom, not separate consonant and vowel letters. This avoids the consonant|vowel cut
   inside a syllable, which is where the current boundaries fail (see `LOCAL_MODELS_AND_TTS.md` section 7).
2. First inventory: about **800 units for 90% of words** (all 36 x 13 CV syllables, the chillus, the standalone vowels, and the frequent conjuncts);
   about 1,100 for 95%.
3. Test before committing: compose words from isolated units and measure whether the composed word matches the real word better than other words
   (rank among distractors). Only then decide whether to record the inventory with real speakers (about 800-1,100 short recordings).
4. Keep the existing whole-word matching as the fallback for the uncovered words.

## Part E. Checking the "576 + conjuncts = about 900-1,000" estimate (2026-10-13)

Measured on the lexicon (230,707 words parsed):

| Quantity | Estimate | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| Consonant + vowel syllables | 36 x 16 = 576 | 522 distinct (13 vowel signs are used, plus the inherent a; ം and ഃ add variants) |
| Chillu letters | 5 | 7 (incl. ൿ and rare ones) |
| Standalone vowels | 15 | 21 (with ം / ഃ forms) |
| Conjunct clusters (bare, vowel sign removed) | "300+" | **1,028 distinct**; but the top 100 cover 93% and the top 300 cover 99.4% of cluster use |
| Distinct written syllable units | 900-1,000 glyphs | **3,727** (each cluster also takes the vowel signs: ക്ക, ക്കാ, ക്കി, ക്കു ...) |

So 900-1,000 is a good number for the *core glyph set* but not for sound units, because a cluster combines with every vowel sign.

**Splitting a cluster into a hold + a syllable** (ക്കു = k-hold + ku; ന്ത = n-hold + tha; clusters with ya/ra/la/va such as പ്ര, ത്ര, സ്വ, വ്യ stay whole) cuts the inventory:

| Words buildable | whole-akshara units | hold + syllable units |
|---|---|---|
| 90% | 788 | **407** |
| 95% | 1,075 | **519** |
| 99% | 2,057 | **786** |
| all parsed words | 3,727 | 1,458 (149 hold units + CV + onset clusters) |

Assumption to test: a geminate or nasal+stop cluster is acoustically a hold followed by the syllable.
Script: `tools/model_eval/akshara_coverage.py` (plus the splitting analysis in this note).

## Part F. The unit inventory built and tested (2026-10-13)

Tool: `tools/model_eval/build_unit_inventory.py` (a conjunct is ONE unit and is never split by default; `S2W_SPLIT_ALL=1` splits every cluster into hold + syllable for comparison experiments only). Output folder
`data\private\units\` (git-ignored): `inventory.json`, `labels.csv`, `labels.bin`, `stats.json`.

**Can the units represent the whole dictionary?** Yes, losslessly, for 99.8% of it. 257,119 of 257,684 words are rebuilt exactly from unit ids
(every one verified by joining the units back into the written word). The other 565 are dictionary typos (stray vowel signs, "അഃി...").

| Inventory | Distinct units | Words covered by the top 300 / 500 units | Units for 90% / 95% / 99% of words |
|---|---|---|---|
| keep ya/ra/la/va onset clusters whole | 1,489 | | 415 / 526 / 797 |
| **split every cluster into hold + syllable** | **837** | | **296 / 351 / 473** |

837 units = 652 syllables (consonant + vowel sign, with ം/ഃ forms) + 35 holds + 22 vowels + 7 chillus + 121 other (3-consonant remainders).
Mean 6.45 units per word.

**Size of the labels for the whole dictionary:**

| Form | Size |
|---|---|
| `labels.bin` (uint16 unit ids + uint32 word offsets, all 257,119 words) | **4.35 MB** (1.66 M unit ids = 3.3 MB, offsets 1.0 MB) |
| `labels.csv` (word, space-separated ids) | 13.5 MB |
| `inventory.json` (id, text, kind, uses) | 61 KB |
| the unit audio, 837 clips, mean 0.41 s, total 5.7 min | 32 MB as generated (44.1 kHz); about 11 MB at 16 kHz mono 16-bit |
| per-clip spectral features (about 40 frames x 13 MFCC, fp16) | about 1 KB per unit, under 1 MB total |

For comparison the full lexicon with meanings and pronunciation is 126 MB.

**Building the sounds with TTS (indic-parler-tts, Anjali, cap + full stop + 200 ms trim), checked by the `indic-core` round trip:**

| Kind | Units | Exact | Unit text found in what was heard |
|---|---|---|---|
| syllable | 652 | 34% | 40% |
| vowel | 22 | 59% | 64% |
| chillu | 7 | 0% | 29% |
| hold (ക് ത് ...) | 35 | 0% | 20% |
| remaining onset clusters | 121 | 0% | 2% |
| **all** | **837** | **28%** | **34%** |

Weighted by how often each unit occurs in the dictionary, about **50% of unit tokens have a clip the recogniser accepts**. Typical misses are
length confusions on isolated short vowels (കു heard as കൂ, തി as തീ, മാ as മ) and look-alike letters (ട / ദ, ണ / ന, ര / റ). Conclusion: the
**labels are sound, the TTS-made sounds are not yet reliable**; isolated syllables and holds are exactly what TTS is worst at. The
inventory needs real recordings (837 prompts, about 15 minutes of speech) or a human pass over these clips with failures re-recorded.

## Part G. Checking in the recorder how the unit sounds represent a word

Start the recorder with `--unit-audio-dir` (the documented Docker command mounts `units_anjali_trim` as `/units`). For the selected word the page
shows, under the AI reference voices, a **"Sounds that build this word"** row:

* one chip per unit (`tools/s2w_units.py` splits the word), with a play button and what the recogniser heard from that unit alone
  (✓ exact, ✗ with the heard text, "no clip" when the unit was never generated);
* **Play composed word** and **Graph (units glued together)**: the unit clips concatenated with 2 ms fades; the graph shows the waveform with each
  unit as a segment and the boundaries are exact (where each clip starts and ends), so you can see and play each unit and the whole;
* **Test: how close to the real word?**: DTW distance (mean-removed MFCC) between the composed word and this word's whole-word reference clips,
  compared with every other word's reference clips: "closer than N of M other words, rank r". Rank 1 means the composed word is nearest the right word.

Example, `amgakaranangalh`: composed from 8 units, distance 32.7 vs 40 for a typical other word, closer than 19 of 19 other words (rank 1).
Only a handful of words have whole-word reference clips (40), so this is a sanity check, not an accuracy measure.

## Part H. Why isolated units "do not match", and the test that matters (2026-10-13)

**Why ങ് + ങ and not ങ്ങ.** The inventory splits every cluster into a hold plus the final syllable (ങ്ങ = ങ് + ങ) to keep it small (837 units
instead of 1,489). The two units spell the word exactly, but a doubled nasal is really one long sound, so keeping frequent clusters whole
(ക്ക, ത്ത, പ്പ, ന്ന, ട്ട, ങ്ങ ...) would be more faithful; it costs more units (the 1,489-unit variant) and has not been generated or tested.

**Why a unit "does not match" (ഗ heard as ഗേ).** Same 50 most-used units, three voices, `indic-core` round trip:

| Voice | exact (of 50) |
|---|---|
| Parler Anjali | 17 |
| Parler Harish | 13 |
| LFM | 15 |
| at least one of the three | 24 |

The wrong answers are the same across voices and run in one direction, short to long: ക→കാ, അ→ആ, കു→കൂ, തി→തീ, നി→നീ, മാ→മ. A syllable spoken
alone (0.3-0.4 s, no context) is pronounced and heard long; that is a property of isolated syllables, not of one voice. "Heard exactly the same
text" is therefore a harsh test for a unit. The test that matters is whether units glued into a word pick out the right word.

**Composed-template word recognition** (`tools/model_eval/compose_rank.py`; units = Parler Anjali; 209 candidate words, each a template
glued from its unit clips and time-normalised to the query, DTW on mean-removed MFCC):

| Queries | n | top-1 | top-5 | MRR | median rank of 209 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LFM voice, clean words (a different voice from the units) | 189 | 12.2% | 36.0% | 0.26 | 9 |
| Parler Anjali, rare words (same voice as the units) | 10 | 40% | 60% | 0.50 | 4 |
| Parler Anjali, everyday words | 10 | 10% | 60% | 0.27 | 5 |

Chance top-1 is 0.5%. So the composed words carry real information (24x chance on a different voice) but are far from good enough alone.
Ideas to try, in order: units cut out of real words (in-context length and coarticulation) instead of spoken alone; trim the hum tail from unit
clips; real recordings of the units by the same speakers; use composed matching to shortlist and a whole-word template to decide.

## Part I. Three ways to get from a spoken word to a dictionary word, same queries (2026-10-13)

150 whole-word clips (MMS-TTS-mal voice) against the same 599 candidate dictionary words (random lexicon words, 2-7 syllables, English meaning).
Chance for rank 1 is 0.17%. Scripts: `mms_cut_units.py`, `mms_rank.py`, `asr_match.py` in `tools/model_eval/`.

| Method | Queries answered | Right word ranked 1st | In top 5 | Median rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Units spoken alone (Parler), glued, DTW | 150 | 0.7% | 2.0% | 230 |
| Units cut out of other words (MMS, leave-one-word-out), glued, DTW | 112 (units with another instance) | 3.6% | 8.0% | 124 |
| **Neural recogniser (indic-core) + nearest dictionary word** | 150 | **41.3%** | **54.0%** | **5** |

* Cutting units out of words is about 5x better than speaking them alone (0.7% to 3.6%), as expected, but both are far below a neural recogniser.
* The recogniser's text is exactly the right word only 5.3% of the time (many of these are rare, obscure words), yet the nearest dictionary word is right 41%
  of the time, because small spelling errors still land close to the right entry.
* Next steps for voice-to-word: rescore the top candidates with the recogniser's own likelihood, add a word-frequency prior, and test with real voices.
* The unit inventory is for generating word audio from letters (synthesis); it is not a competitive way to recognise words.
Caveat: queries are one synthetic voice; nothing here is about real speakers.
